# Emberwood — story bible

## The world in one paragraph

Two centuries ago the Ember Network — a chain of signal beacons powered by
Ember Cells — stitched the scattered settlements of a healing world together.
Then came the Quiet: one by one the beacons went dark, and nobody now alive
remembers why. Emberwood is a small settlement built into the bones of an old
research station, huddled around the last beacon anyone bothered to maintain.
When its three Ember Cells were scattered — stolen, lost, and sealed away —
the dark crept back in, and with it the sludge.

## Timeline

- **The Verdant Century** — Station EMB-R-WD ("Emberwood") is founded as a
  terraforming research post: a bio-dome for crop engineering ("the Eden
  Shelf"), a foundry mine casting Ember Cells, a coolant canal feeding both.
- **The Bloom Error** — The dome's bio-remediation strain, designed to digest
  industrial waste, turns out to digest *containment* too. The strain is
  flushed into the deep foundry galleries and the dome is sealed. The orange
  sludge in the mine is its descendant; it still glows with the Ember
  charge it was bred to feed on. (The big one has been feeding a long time.)
- **The Quiet** — Beacons upstream go dark over a single winter. Freight
  drones stop coming. The station's staff walk out to find answers; most
  never return. The ones who stay become the first settlers.
- **Now** — Overseer Rowan keeps the old protocols alive. The beacon's three
  cells are gone: one carried into the overgrowth by Keeper Ivy's
  predecessor "for safekeeping", one lost on the canal isle in a ferry
  accident, one sealed in the foundry behind a keycard door when the Bloom
  Error hit. The player — a wandering engineer — walks in the night the
  beacon dies completely.

## People

- **Overseer Rowan** (they/them) — last station-line officer. Collects the
  scattered **archive logs** to reconstruct why the Quiet happened. Dry,
  formal, secretly sentimental.
- **Keeper Ivy** — hermit botanist living inside the Eden Shelf. Their
  predecessor hid Cell #1 in the overgrowth; Ivy tends the dome like a
  garden-shrine and knows the sludge is not evil, just hungry.
- **Old Finn** — canal angler; married fifty years, widowed ten. The ring is
  the only thing left of Bea. Trades the foundry keycard because he never
  wants to see the place again — he worked the casting line as a boy.
- **MARO-7** — merchant droid, the last freight unit from before the Quiet.
  Sells what it salvages. Its awning antenna still listens for a network
  that never answers — which is why it cares whether the beacon lights.
- **Pip Harroway** — settler kid. Their pet drone **Bolt** chased a glow into
  the overgrowth and never came home.
- **Mara Harroway** — Pip's mother, archivist by stubbornness. Keeps one of
  the three archive logs at home.

## Quest structure

**Main**: recover the 3 Ember Cells → reignite the beacon.
- Cell 1 — Eden Shelf overgrowth maze (chest; Ivy's predecessor's cache)
- Cell 2 — Runoff isle (chest; ferry wreck salvage)
- Cell 3 — Foundry Gallery, behind the keycard blast door + the big sludge

**Side quests** (all optional, all flag-driven):
1. **Bea's ring** — find the ring in the shore dust → Finn trades the foundry
   keycard. (Gates the main quest's third cell; already shipped.)
2. **Bolt come home** — find Pip's pet drone Bolt in the Eden Shelf →
   return it → Bolt hovers beside Pip in the plaza forever after; +8 scrap.
3. **The canal filter** — after Finn hands over the keycard he asks for a
   spare pump filter from the foundry's lower gallery → +20 scrap and
   Finn's gratitude.
4. **The archive logs** — three data terminals (Eden Shelf, Foundry lower
   gallery, the Harroways') each hold a log. Read all three → tell Rowan →
   Rowan pieces together the truth of the Quiet (epilogue lore) + 10 scrap.

## The three archive logs (the truth of the Quiet, in pieces)

- **LOG A — Eden Shelf, agronomy terminal**: the Bloom Error was not an
  accident. The strain was *ordered* to be made hungrier — the network
  upstream demanded double cell output, and waste digestion was the
  bottleneck.
- **LOG B — Foundry gallery terminal**: the foundry's last shift log. Cells
  shipped upstream were coming back *drained* — something on the network was
  pulling charge out faster than any settlement could use it.
- **LOG C — Harroway family archive**: a letter, never sent, from the
  station's signal officer: the upstream beacons didn't fail — they were
  *switched off* to starve whatever was draining the network. The Quiet was
  a quarantine. Keeping one beacon lit was Emberwood's choice: "Someone has
  to leave a light on for the ones still out there."

Rowan's synthesis (on returning all three): Emberwood's beacon isn't a relic
— it's a promise, and tonight the player kept it.

## Tone rules

Dusk-warm, hopeful post-collapse. Nobody is a villain: the sludge is an
abandoned tool, the Quiet was a sacrifice, the droid misses its network.
Dialogue is short, concrete, a little wry. No proper nouns without a person
attached to them.
